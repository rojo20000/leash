Your day at the front desk

  1. Open Schedule. Everyone due today is listed by time.
  2. When someone arrives, press Check in, confirm their date of birth and insurance card, then check them in. The clinician sees them as waiting straight away.
  3. Book new appointments on the right. Only open times can be picked.
  4. Switch to tomorrow to prepare reminders. Copy each one into your usual texting or portal tool, then mark it sent.

What you can see

Names, dates of birth, contact details, insurance and appointment times. Clinical records are not available to the front desk, and the front desk assistant only knows the schedule. Clinical questions go to a nurse or clinician.

Your job here

  1. Open the Audit log. Every sign-in, record opened, answer and change is listed, newest first.
  2. Check the line at the top says the log is intact. It is hash-chained, so an edited or deleted line is detected.
  3. Random quality checks of assistant answers wait for you in Needs review. Mark each one fine or a problem.
  4. Conversations shows every question asked of the assistant, with what changed afterwards.

You can't open patient records or ask the assistant. That keeps the review independent.

Your job here

  1. Open Network. It shows every service the app talks to and whether each one is up.
  2. The app never calls out to the internet. The outbound panel lists the checks that show it.
  3. If the local model is down, answers fall back to practice mode. Restart Ollama on this computer.

You see connections only, never patient data.

How this works

  1. Open a patient. The list starts with whoever has the most to check.
  2. Ask about them in the panel beside the record, or tap one of the suggested questions. Names and contact details are taken out before the model sees anything.
  3. Read the label on the answer before you use it.
  4. As clinical lead, you also decide on stopped requests in Needs review.

Answer labels

Ready to read
Nothing flagged. Still a draft for you to check.
Check first
Something needs your attention, such as a possible emergency. You read why, then open the answer.
Stopped
Not shown. A clinical lead looks at it; nothing for you to do.

The assistant never contacts anyone. A message it drafts goes out only after a clinician approves it and a person sends it.
